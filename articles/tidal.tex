\title{A Reduced Newtonian Model of Tidally Deformable Pseudo-Fluid Planets:
Full Modal Formulation and Quadrupole Implementation}

\author{Jan~Ol\v{s}ina\\[1ex] Independent Researcher, Prague, Czech Republic\\
email: \texttt{jan.olsina@gmail.com},  ORCID: \texttt{0009-0003-9816-3958}.}



\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
This paper develops a Newtonian model for tidally interacting planets whose
surfaces deviate from exact sphericity through damped pseudo-fluid modes.
The goal is not a full hydrodynamic interior simulation, but a structured
reduced model that still contains the key mechanisms of tidal evolution:
tidal bulges, gravitational backreaction of the bulges, spin torques,
orbital circularization, spin synchronization, and obliquity evolution.
First, a general deformable-body formulation is written in which each planet
has orbital motion, rigid-body orientation, spin, and an arbitrary set of
surface modes. Second, a feasible all-mode approximation is introduced using
body-frame symmetric trace-free tensors and generalized rheology with
relaxation branches. Third, a quadrupole truncation is derived and written
in a form directly suitable for numerical integration. Compared with the
simplest quadrupole tide model, the upgraded version includes
shape-dependent spin inertia, rotational flattening, frequency-dependent
lag through internal memory variables, and the leading quadrupole angular
momentum carried by the deformation itself. This last correction restores the
explicit quadrupole torque to the reduced spin equation without upsetting the
conservative bookkeeping, making the model materially better suited to
obliquity studies.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Tides, celestial mechanics, deformable bodies, spherical harmonics,
quadrupole approximation, spin--orbit coupling, rheology.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}

Many problems in celestial mechanics lie between two standard idealizations.
At one extreme, planets are treated as point masses and evolve only through
Newtonian $N$-body gravity. At the other extreme, one solves the full
equations of fluid or viscoelastic motion in self-gravitating rotating
bodies, including stratification, realistic rheology, and thermal physics.
The first approach is simple but cannot represent tides. The second is
physically rich but expensive and often problem-specific.

The purpose of this work is to formulate a middle-ground model with the
following properties:
\begin{enumerate}
\item full three-dimensional Newtonian orbital dynamics,
\item explicit deformation degrees of freedom,
\item deformation-dependent gravity,
\item spin and orientation dynamics,
\item dissipative tidal lag,
\item a systematic path from a general modal model to a compact
implementation model.
\end{enumerate}

The resulting framework is still a reduced model---much closer to a
``structured toy model'' than to a full planetary interior simulation.
It is not an arbitrary toy, however: the couplings between orbit, spin, and
deformation have the correct geometric form, and increasingly refined
approximations can be introduced without changing the overall architecture.

Four specific improvements distinguish the present version from the simplest
damped-quadrupole model. The spin inertia depends on the tidal deformation.
Rotational flattening is handled through the same shape-dependent coefficient
that enters the spin kinetic energy, so the rotational forcing and the energy
bookkeeping share a single common parameter. The dissipative response supports
one or more relaxation branches rather than a single constant time-lag term,
allowing frequency-dependent rheology while remaining in ordinary differential
equation (ODE) form. Finally, the quadrupole closure retains the leading
angular momentum carried by the quadrupole mode itself; this is the minimal
correction that allows the explicit quadrupole torque to appear in the spin
equation without spoiling conservative bookkeeping, and it is especially
important for obliquity evolution.

\section{Kinematics and Variables}

Consider $N$ planets indexed by $a=1,\dots,N$. For each body we introduce
\begin{itemize}
\item mass $M_a$,
\item reference radius $R_a$,
\item center-of-mass position $X_a(t)\in\mathbb{R}^3$,
\item orientation matrix $Q_a(t)\in SO(3)$,
\item body-frame angular velocity $\Omega_a(t)\in\mathbb{R}^3$.
\end{itemize}

The orientation evolves according to
\begin{equation}
\dot Q_a = Q_a \widehat{\Omega}_a,
\label{eq:Qdot}
\end{equation}
where $\widehat{\Omega}_a$ is the skew-symmetric matrix satisfying
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\Omega}_a v = \Omega_a \times v.
\end{equation}

A point $y$ in the body frame corresponds to inertial position
\begin{equation}
x = X_a + Q_a y.
\label{eq:body-to-inertial}
\end{equation}

This explicit separation between inertial and body frames is essential. It
avoids fictitious torques in the reduced equations and makes it possible to
describe tides by body-frame variables while still computing forces and
torques in the inertial frame.

\section{Surface Description and Exact Deformable Body}

\subsection{Surface shape}

Assume the body remains star-shaped with respect to its center. Its surface
in the body frame is written as
\begin{equation}
r = \mathcal{R}_a(\hat y,t)
   = R_a\bigl(1+u_a(\hat y,t)\bigr),
\label{eq:surface}
\end{equation}
where $\hat y \in S^2$ and $u_a$ is the dimensionless shape field.

Expand the shape in spherical harmonics:
\begin{equation}
u_a(\hat y,t)
=
\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}
\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}
q_{a\ell m}(t)\,Y_{\ell m}(\hat y).
\label{eq:u-spherical}
\end{equation}

The occupied body-frame volume is
\begin{equation}
V_a(t)
=
\left\{
y\in\mathbb{R}^3:
0\le |y|<\mathcal{R}_a(\hat y,t)
\right\}.
\label{eq:Va}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Constraints}

For an incompressible pseudo-fluid, the volume is fixed:
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{3}\int_{S^2}
\mathcal{R}_a(\hat y,t)^3\,d\Omega
=
\frac{4\pi}{3}R_a^3.
\label{eq:volume}
\end{equation}

The body-frame center of mass is fixed at the origin:
\begin{equation}
\int_{V_a(t)} y\,d^3y = 0.
\label{eq:center}
\end{equation}

At linear order, these conditions eliminate independent $\ell=0$ and
$\ell=1$ modes. The tidal content therefore begins at $\ell=2$.

\subsection{Exact gravity}

Assume uniform density inside the deformed body:
\begin{equation}
\rho_a(t)=\frac{M_a}{\mathrm{Vol}(V_a(t))}.
\end{equation}

The exact Newtonian potential of body $a$ is
\begin{equation}
\Phi_a(x,t)
=
-G\rho_a
\int_{V_a(t)}
\frac{d^3y}{|x-X_a-Q_a y|}.
\label{eq:exactPhi}
\end{equation}

For $x$ outside the body, this admits a multipole expansion. Let
\begin{equation}
s = Q_a^{\top}(x-X_a).
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
\Phi_a(x,t)
=
-G
\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}
\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}
\frac{4\pi}{2\ell+1}
\frac{Q_{a\ell m}(t)}{|s|^{\ell+1}}
Y_{\ell m}(\hat s),
\label{eq:multipolePhi}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
Q_{a\ell m}(t)
=
\int_{V_a(t)}
\rho_a\,
r^\ell
Y_{\ell m}^{*}(\hat y)\,d^3y.
\label{eq:exactQlm}
\end{equation}

Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
Q_{a\ell m}(t)
=
\frac{\rho_a}{\ell+3}
\int_{S^2}
\mathcal{R}_a(\hat y,t)^{\ell+3}
Y_{\ell m}^{*}(\hat y)\,d\Omega.
\label{eq:QlmSurface}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Exact pseudo-fluid motion}

Let the internal motion be irrotational in the body frame:
\begin{equation}
w_a(y,t)=\nabla_y \phi_a(y,t),
\qquad
\nabla_y^2 \phi_a = 0
\quad \text{in }V_a(t).
\label{eq:phi}
\end{equation}

The inertial velocity is then
\begin{equation}
v_a(x,t)
=
\dot X_a + Q_a \bigl(\Omega_a \times y + \nabla_y \phi_a \bigr).
\label{eq:v-inertial}
\end{equation}

If
\begin{equation}
F_a(y,t)=|y|-\mathcal{R}_a(\hat y,t),
\end{equation}
the kinematic boundary condition is
\begin{equation}
\partial_t F_a + \nabla_y F_a\cdot \nabla_y \phi_a = 0
\quad \text{on }F_a=0.
\label{eq:kinematic}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Exact energies}

The orbital kinetic energy is
\begin{equation}
T_{\mathrm{orb}}
=
\sum_a \frac{1}{2} M_a |\dot X_a|^2.
\label{eq:Torb}
\end{equation}

The internal-plus-spin kinetic energy is
\begin{equation}
T_{\mathrm{int+spin}}
=
\sum_a
\frac{\rho_a}{2}
\int_{V_a(t)}
\left|
\Omega_a\times y + \nabla\phi_a
\right|^2 d^3y.
\label{eq:Tspinfull}
\end{equation}

The self-gravity is
\begin{equation}
U_a^{\mathrm{self}}
=
-\frac{G\rho_a^2}{2}
\int_{V_a(t)}\int_{V_a(t)}
\frac{d^3y\,d^3z}{|y-z|}.
\label{eq:Uself}
\end{equation}

A material or restoring energy may be added as a functional of the shape:
\begin{equation}
U_a^{\mathrm{mat}} = U_a^{\mathrm{mat}}[u_a].
\label{eq:Umat}
\end{equation}

The exact mutual interaction is
\begin{equation}
U_{ab}
=
-G\rho_a\rho_b
\int_{V_a(t)}\int_{V_b(t)}
\frac{d^3y\,d^3z}
{|X_a-X_b+Q_a y-Q_b z|}.
\label{eq:UabExact}
\end{equation}

The exact Lagrangian is
\begin{equation}
L
=
T_{\mathrm{orb}} + T_{\mathrm{int+spin}}
-\sum_a \bigl(U_a^{\mathrm{self}}+U_a^{\mathrm{mat}}\bigr)
-\sum_{a<b}U_{ab}.
\label{eq:Lexact}
\end{equation}

This exact moving-boundary model is conceptually useful, but not practical
as a general-purpose tide integrator. The moving-domain Laplace
problem, the nonlinear volume constraints, and the double volume integrals
make it far more expensive than desired. The rest of the paper develops a
feasible approximation that keeps the same orbit--spin--shape structure.

\section{Feasible Full Modal Model}

\subsection{STF mode variables}

For actual computation, it is more convenient to replace the spherical
harmonic amplitudes by body-frame symmetric trace-free (STF) tensors.
For each degree $\ell\ge2$, let
\begin{equation}
A_a^{\langle L\rangle}(t)
\end{equation}
be a body-frame STF tensor of rank $\ell$. This is equivalent to the set of
coefficients $\{q_{a\ell m}\}_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}$.

The corresponding mass multipole is written as
\begin{equation}
M_a^{\langle L\rangle}
=
\kappa_{a\ell} A_a^{\langle L\rangle}.
\label{eq:mass-multipole}
\end{equation}

For later use, recall that for any rank-2 tensor $B$ its symmetric
trace-free part is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{STF}(B)
=
\frac{1}{2}(B+B^\top)
-
\frac{1}{3}(\mathrm{tr}\,B)I_3.
\label{eq:STFdef}
\end{equation}
In particular, since $\Omega_a\Omega_a^\top$ is already symmetric, the
symmetrization step in \eqref{eq:STFdef} is redundant and one simply has
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{STF}(\Omega_a\Omega_a^\top)
=
\Omega_a\Omega_a^\top
-
\frac{1}{3}|\Omega_a|^2 I_3.
\label{eq:STFOmega}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Corotational derivative}

Because the modes are stored in the rotating body frame, the natural time
derivative is the corotational derivative
\begin{equation}
(D_t A)_{i_1\dots i_\ell}
=
\dot A_{i_1\dots i_\ell}
+
\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}
\epsilon_{i_k p q}\,
\Omega_{a,p}\,
A_{i_1\dots q \dots i_\ell}.
\label{eq:DtA}
\end{equation}

Its second derivative is
\begin{equation}
D_t^2 A = D_t(D_t A).
\label{eq:Dt2A}
\end{equation}

\subsection{External tidal moments}

Let $\Phi_{\mathrm{ext},a}$ be the potential generated by all bodies other
than $a$. The inertial STF tidal moments at $X_a$ are
\begin{equation}
E_{a,\mathrm{in}}^{\langle L\rangle}
=
\left[
\nabla^{\langle L\rangle}
\Phi_{\mathrm{ext},a}(x)
\right]_{x=X_a}.
\label{eq:E-in-full}
\end{equation}

The body-frame moments are
\begin{equation}
E_{a,\mathrm{body}}^{\langle L\rangle}
=
Q_a^{\top}\cdots Q_a^{\top}
E_{a,\mathrm{in}}^{\langle L\rangle}.
\label{eq:E-body-full}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Shape-dependent spin inertia}

A key improvement over the simplest tide models is that the spin inertia is
allowed to depend on the deformation. Denote the body-frame spin inertia by
\begin{equation}
J_a = J_a[A].
\label{eq:J-of-A}
\end{equation}

For small deformations, this may be expanded linearly in the retained modes.
In particular, in the quadrupole sector one will use
\begin{equation}
J_a(S_a)
=
J_{a0} I_3 - c_{J,a} S_a + O(S_a^2).
\label{eq:Jlinear}
\end{equation}
Though this form is sufficient at the present level, an effective coefficient
will be introduced later in the reduced quadrupole closure to absorb the
contribution from rotational flattening.

This correction is important for leading-order consistency between tidal
torques and spin dynamics. In the reduced quadrupole closure developed below,
the same shape dependence is also used to represent the retained
rotational-flattening response, rather than introducing a second independent
conservative storage term at the same order.

\subsection{Generalized rheology}

A single linear damping term corresponds essentially to a constant
time-lag response. To allow more flexible frequency dependence while keeping
the system in ODE form, introduce memory variables
\begin{equation}
Z_{a\ell n}^{\langle L\rangle}(t),
\qquad
n=1,\dots,N_{\mathrm{r},a\ell},
\label{eq:Zdef}
\end{equation}
where $n$ indexes relaxation branches.

Each branch has stiffness $c_{a\ell n}\ge0$ and relaxation time
$\tau_{a\ell n}>0$.

\subsection{Reduced full-model Lagrangian}

A convenient all-mode reduced Lagrangian is
\begin{align}
L
=&
\sum_a
\Bigg[
\frac{1}{2} M_a |\dot X_a|^2
+
\frac{1}{2}\Omega_a^\top J_a[A]\Omega_a
\nonumber\\
&\qquad
+
\sum_{\ell\ge2}
\frac{\mu_{a\ell}}{2}
\bigl\|D_t A_a^{\langle L\rangle}\bigr\|^2
-
\sum_{\ell\ge2}
\frac{\mu_{a\ell}\omega_{a\ell}^2}{2}
\bigl\|A_a^{\langle L\rangle}\bigr\|^2
\Bigg]\nonumber\\
&-U(X,Q,A),
\label{eq:Lreduced}
\end{align}
where $\mu_{a\ell}$ is a modal inertia and $\omega_{a\ell}$ is the reference
mode frequency.

The interaction energy is approximated by
\begin{equation}
U
=
-\sum_{a<b}
\frac{G M_a M_b}{|X_a-X_b|}
+
U_{\mathrm{tide}}
+
U_{\mathrm{rot}},
\label{eq:Ureduced}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
U_{\mathrm{tide}}
=
-\sum_a \sum_{\ell\ge2}
\frac{1}{\ell!}
M_{a,\mathrm{in}}^{\langle L\rangle}
E_{a,\mathrm{in}}^{\langle L\rangle},
\label{eq:Utide}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
U_{\mathrm{rot}}
=
\sum_a \beta_{a2}\,
A_a^{\langle ij\rangle}
\mathrm{STF}\!\left(\Omega_{ai}\Omega_{aj}\right),
\label{eq:UrotReduced}
\end{equation}
where only the $\ell=2$ sector contributes. At the full modal level this term
is the conservative rotational coupling whose gradient with respect to the
quadrupole mode produces the centrifugal forcing retained in the shape
equation.

\subsection{All-mode evolution equations}

The reduced full-model equations are
\begin{equation}
M_a \ddot X_a = -\nabla_{X_a}U,
\label{eq:Xfull}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\dot Q_a = Q_a \widehat{\Omega}_a,
\label{eq:Qfull}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\dot \Pi_a + \Omega_a \times \Pi_a = \tau_a,
\qquad
\Pi_a = \frac{\partial L}{\partial \Omega_a},
\label{eq:spinfull}
\end{equation}
where $\Pi_a$ is the total body-frame angular momentum retained by the model.

For the shape modes, use
\begin{align}
\mu_{a\ell} D_t^2 A_a^{\langle L\rangle}
&+
\mu_{a\ell}\omega_{a\ell}^2 A_a^{\langle L\rangle}
+
\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\mathrm{r},a\ell}}
c_{a\ell n}
\bigl(
A_a^{\langle L\rangle}
-
Z_{a\ell n}^{\langle L\rangle}
\bigr)
\nonumber\\
&=
-\kappa_{a\ell}
E_{a,\mathrm{body}}^{\langle L\rangle}
-
\delta_{\ell 2}\,
\beta_{a2}\,
\mathrm{STF}
\bigl(
\Omega_a\Omega_a^\top
\bigr)^{\langle L\rangle}.
\label{eq:Aeq}
\end{align}

The relaxation branches satisfy
\begin{equation}
\tau_{a\ell n}\,
D_t Z_{a\ell n}^{\langle L\rangle}
=
A_a^{\langle L\rangle}
-
Z_{a\ell n}^{\langle L\rangle}.
\label{eq:Zeq}
\end{equation}

Equation \eqref{eq:Aeq} contains three distinct sources of deformation:
the body's own restoring tendency, the external tide, and the centrifugal
flattening term in the $\ell=2$ sector. In the rank-2 case, the rotational
forcing is exactly the STF tensor defined in \eqref{eq:STFOmega}, which
removes the isotropic part and retains only the pure quadrupolar flattening.
In the full modal model this rotational term may be viewed as arising from the
conservative coupling $U_{\mathrm{rot}}$. In the reduced quadrupole closure used
later, the same effect is folded into the shape-dependent inertia coefficient
so that the reduced spin equation and the reduced energy remain consistent.

\subsection{Comments on angular momentum}

The angular-momentum bookkeeping of the reduced model deserves emphasis,
because it is the point where a naive implementation most easily goes wrong.
The reduced model is most consistent when the spin equation is derived from
the same Lagrangian that defines the mode kinetic energy. In that case
$\Pi_a$ includes not only the rigid-spin part $J_a[A]\Omega_a$, but also the
angular momentum carried by the corotational mode kinetic terms. For the
quadrupole reduction developed below, retaining the leading contribution of
that modal angular momentum turns out to be essential: it is the minimal
correction that allows the explicit quadrupole torque to act in the spin
balance without spoiling the conservative bookkeeping. Higher-order modal and
higher-multipole contributions to $\Pi_a$ may still be neglected in a reduced
implementation, but the leading quadrupole piece should be kept whenever one
wants the reduced model to represent secular obliquity evolution credibly.

\section{Physical Interpretation of the Full Modal Model}

The full modal model contains the main qualitative tidal effects.

\subsection{Tidal bulges}

In the slow-forcing limit, the modes approach a quasi-static response
proportional to the external tidal field:
\begin{equation}
A_a^{\langle L\rangle}
\approx
-\frac{\kappa_{a\ell}}
{\mu_{a\ell}\omega_{a\ell}^2}
E_{a,\mathrm{body}}^{\langle L\rangle},
\label{eq:Astatic}
\end{equation}
up to corrections from rheology and rotation. This is the tidal bulge.

\subsection{Rotational flattening}

Even without an external perturber, the reduced energy contains the
spin--shape coupling
\begin{equation}
U_{\mathrm{rot},a}
=
\beta_{a2}\,A_a^{\langle ij\rangle}
\mathrm{STF}\!\left(\Omega_{ai}\Omega_{aj}\right),
\end{equation}
whose gradient generates the term
\begin{equation}
-\delta_{\ell2}\,\beta_{a2}\,
\mathrm{STF}
\bigl(
\Omega_a\Omega_a^\top
\bigr)
\end{equation}
in \eqref{eq:Aeq}. Thus an isolated rapidly rotating body does not relax to a
perfect sphere; instead it approaches the rotationally flattened equilibrium
selected by this conservative coupling, modified by any dissipation present.

\subsection{Lag, synchronization, and circularization}

The relaxation branches cause the deformation to lag behind the forcing. The
lagged bulge exerts a torque on the spin and modifies the force on the orbit.
As a result, the model supports spin synchronization or pseudo-synchronization,
obliquity damping, orbital circularization, and exchange of angular momentum
between orbit, spin, and tides. In the quadrupole truncation developed in the
next section, these effects arise from the combined action of the lagged shape
tensor, the explicit quadrupole torque, and the modal angular momentum
$\Pi_{S,a}$ retained in the spin balance.

\section{Quadrupole Reduction}

The most useful implementation model keeps only the $\ell=2$ sector.

\subsection{Quadrupole shape tensor}

Represent the body-frame quadrupole deformation by a symmetric trace-free
matrix
\begin{equation}
S_a(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3},
\qquad
S_a = S_a^\top,
\qquad
\mathrm{tr}\,S_a = 0.
\label{eq:S}
\end{equation}

The perturbed surface is
\begin{equation}
r = R_a \bigl(1+\hat n^\top S_a \hat n\bigr).
\label{eq:surfaceS}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Quadrupole moment and inertia}

The inertial mass quadrupole is
\begin{equation}
I_a
=
c_{Q,a}\,Q_a S_a Q_a^\top.
\label{eq:Idef}
\end{equation}
For the normalization used in many reduced models,
\begin{equation}
c_{Q,a}=\frac{2}{5}M_aR_a^2.
\label{eq:cQ}
\end{equation}

The body-frame spin inertia is taken as
\begin{equation}
J_a(S_a)
=
J_{a0} I_3 - \tilde c_{J,a} S_a,
\label{eq:JS}
\end{equation}
where $J_{a0}$ is the spherical reference inertia and $\tilde c_{J,a}$ is the
effective reduced spin--shape coefficient. If one starts from a split into a
direct inertia-response coefficient $c_{J,a}$ and a separate conservative
rotational-flattening coefficient $\alpha_a$, then the reduced quadrupole
closure requires the combination
\begin{equation}
\tilde c_{J,a}=c_{J,a}+2\alpha_a.
\label{eq:cJeff}
\end{equation}
For a homogeneous reference body,
\begin{equation}
J_{a0}=\frac{2}{5}M_aR_a^2.
\label{eq:J0}
\end{equation}

Equation \eqref{eq:JS} is a linearized leading-order correction. Because
$S_a$ is trace-free, this closure keeps $\mathrm{tr}\,J_a=3J_{a0}$ fixed. That
should be understood as a small-deformation linearization: $O(S_a^2)$
corrections would in general modify the trace, so \eqref{eq:JS} is not exact
for large deformation. Within the reduced quadrupole closure, however,
\eqref{eq:JS} is the correct place to retain the leading rotational-flattening
feedback, because the same coefficient must appear both in the spin kinetic
energy and in the rotational forcing of the shape equation.

\subsection{External tidal tensor}

Let
\begin{equation}
D_{ab}=X_b-X_a,
\quad
d_{ab}=|D_{ab}|,
\quad
\hat D_{ab}=D_{ab}/d_{ab}.
\label{eq:Dab}
\end{equation}

The inertial quadrupolar tidal tensor produced by the other bodies is
\begin{equation}
E_{a,\mathrm{in}}
=
\sum_{b\neq a}
\frac{G M_b}{d_{ab}^3}
\left(
I_3 - 3\hat D_{ab}\hat D_{ab}^\top
\right).
\label{eq:Ein}
\end{equation}

The body-frame tidal tensor is
\begin{equation}
E_{a,\mathrm{body}}
=
Q_a^\top E_{a,\mathrm{in}} Q_a.
\label{eq:Ebody}
\end{equation}

Because the quadrupole interaction energy is
\begin{equation}
U_{\mathrm{tide},a}^{(2)}
=
+\frac{1}{2} I_a : E_{a,\mathrm{in}}
=
+\frac{c_{Q,a}}{2} S_a : E_{a,\mathrm{body}},
\label{eq:Utidequad}
\end{equation}
the reciprocal tidal forcing that appears in the shape equation is
\begin{equation}
\lambda_a E_{a,\mathrm{body}},
\qquad
\lambda_a \equiv \frac{c_{Q,a}}{2}.
\label{eq:lambdaQ}
\end{equation}
This coefficient is required for consistency between the quadrupole force,
torque, interaction energy, and the internal mode dynamics.


In the reduced quadrupole closure adopted here, rotational flattening is not
kept as a separate stored term $U_{\mathrm{rot},a}^{(2)}$. Instead it is absorbed
into the same linearized inertia coefficient that appears in \eqref{eq:JS}.
Because $S_a$ is symmetric and trace-free,
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{2}\Omega_a^\top J_a(S_a)\Omega_a
=
\frac{1}{2}J_{a0}|\Omega_a|^2
-
\frac{1}{2}\tilde c_{J,a}
S_a:
\mathrm{STF}\!\bigl(\Omega_a\Omega_a^\top\bigr),
\label{eq:Tspinquadsplit}
\end{equation}
so the rotational contribution implied by the reduced spin kinetic energy is
\begin{equation}
-\frac{1}{2}\tilde c_{J,a}\,
\mathrm{STF}
\bigl(
\Omega_a\Omega_a^\top
\bigr).
\label{eq:Urotgrad}
\end{equation}
This is the coefficient that must appear in the reduced quadrupole shape
equation. Adding a second independent term
$\alpha_a S_a:\mathrm{STF}(\Omega_a\Omega_a^\top)$ to the reduced mechanical
energy would double count the same conservative coupling unless one also kept
the corresponding extra contribution to the reduced spin momentum.

\subsection{Corotational derivative}

For any body-frame rank-2 tensor, define
\begin{equation}
D_t S_a
=
\dot S_a + [\widehat{\Omega}_a,S_a],
\label{eq:DtS}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
[A,B]=AB-BA.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
D_t^2 S_a = D_t(D_t S_a).
\label{eq:Dt2S}
\end{equation}

It is convenient to introduce the auxiliary variable
\begin{equation}
W_a = D_t S_a.
\label{eq:Wdef}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Quadrupole shape equation with rheology}

The quadrupole shape equation is
\begin{align}
D_t^2 S_a
&+
2\gamma_a D_t S_a
+
\omega_{a2}^2 S_a
+
\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\mathrm{r},a}}
c_{an}
\bigl(S_a-Z_{an}\bigr)
\nonumber\\
&=
- \lambda_a E_{a,\mathrm{body}}
-
\frac{1}{2}\tilde c_{J,a}
\,\mathrm{STF}
\bigl(
\Omega_a\Omega_a^\top
\bigr).
\label{eq:Seq}
\end{align}

Each relaxation branch obeys
\begin{equation}
\tau_{an}\,D_t Z_{an} = S_a - Z_{an}.
\label{eq:Zquad}
\end{equation}

The coefficient $\gamma_a$ represents direct viscous-like damping of the
quadrupole rate, while the branch terms represent delayed internal response.
A single branch is often enough for a useful toy model; several branches can
approximate a broader frequency-dependent rheology.

\subsection{Spin equation}

For obliquity-sensitive applications, the reduced quadrupole model should
retain the leading angular momentum carried by the quadrupole mode itself.
With
\begin{equation}
W_a=D_tS_a,
\label{eq:Wdefrepeat}
\end{equation}
and with $\operatorname{axl}(\widehat v)=v$ for antisymmetric matrices, the
leading quadrupole modal contribution is
\begin{equation}
\Pi_{S,a}
=
2\,\operatorname{axl}\!\bigl([W_a,S_a]\bigr).
\label{eq:Pimodequad}
\end{equation}
This follows directly from $\Pi_a=\partial L/\partial\Omega_a$ applied to the
quadrupole kinetic term $\tfrac12\|W_a\|^2$.

The same objective-rate dissipative terms that appear in the shape equations
also generate reciprocal body torques. For the direct viscous damping and the
relaxation branches, define
\begin{equation}
N^{\mathrm{diss}}_{W,a}
=
-2\gamma_a\,\Pi_{S,a}
=
-4\gamma_a\,\operatorname{axl}\!\bigl([W_a,S_a]\bigr),
\label{eq:NdissW}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
N^{\mathrm{diss}}_{Z,a}
=
-2\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\mathrm{r},a}}
 c_{an}\,\operatorname{axl}\!\bigl([S_a-Z_{an},Z_{an}]\bigr).
\label{eq:NdissZ}
\end{equation}
These terms vanish in the nondissipative limit and ensure that the same
objective-rate dissipation that damps the internal tensors also removes the
corresponding body-frame angular momentum from the rigid-spin sector.

The improved body-frame quadrupole spin equation is therefore
\begin{align}
\frac{d}{dt}\Bigl(J_a(S_a)\Omega_a+\Pi_{S,a}\Bigr)
+
\Omega_a\times
\Bigl(J_a(S_a)\Omega_a+\Pi_{S,a}\Bigr)
\nonumber \\= 
N_{a,\mathrm{body}}
+
N^{\mathrm{diss}}_{W,a}
+
N^{\mathrm{diss}}_{Z,a}.
\label{eq:spinquad}
\end{align}

Expanding the time derivative gives
\begin{align}
J_a(S_a)\dot\Omega_a
=
N_{a,\mathrm{body}}
+
N^{\mathrm{diss}}_{W,a}
+
N^{\mathrm{diss}}_{Z,a}
-
\dot J_a(S_a)\Omega_a \nonumber \\
-
\dot\Pi_{S,a}
-
\Omega_a\times
\Bigl(J_a(S_a)\Omega_a+\Pi_{S,a}\Bigr).
\label{eq:spinexpanded}
\end{align}
Since $W_a=D_tS_a$, one also has
\begin{equation}
\dot S_a = W_a-[\widehat{\Omega}_a,S_a],
\label{eq:SdotFromW}
\end{equation}
so that in the linearized closure
\begin{equation}
\dot J_a(S_a)=-\tilde c_{J,a}\dot S_a.
\label{eq:JdotS}
\end{equation}
The modal contribution evolves as
\begin{equation}
\dot\Pi_{S,a}
=
2\,\operatorname{axl}\!\bigl([\dot W_a,S_a]+[W_a,\dot S_a]\bigr).
\label{eq:Pimodedot}
\end{equation}
Equations \eqref{eq:spinquad}--\eqref{eq:Pimodedot} explicitly retain the
orientational dynamics carried by the quadrupole mode. The shape dependence of
$J_a(S_a)$ still captures the accompanying change in spin inertia, but
retaining $\Pi_{S,a}$ is required to close the spin--tide angular-momentum
exchange at this order.

\subsection{Quadrupole torque}

The inertial quadrupole torque associated with the same truncated interaction
energy is
\begin{equation}
N_{a,\mathrm{in}}
=
3\sum_{b\neq a}
\frac{G M_b}{d_{ab}^3}
\bigl(I_a\hat D_{ab}\bigr)\times \hat D_{ab}.
\label{eq:Nin}
\end{equation}

The body-frame version is
\begin{equation}
N_{a,\mathrm{body}}
=
Q_a^\top N_{a,\mathrm{in}}.
\label{eq:Nbody}
\end{equation}
In the improved quadrupole closure, \eqref{eq:Nin}--\eqref{eq:Nbody} are not
merely diagnostic: they are the physical right-hand side of
\eqref{eq:spinquad}. Because the spin balance explicitly retains the modal
angular momentum \eqref{eq:Pimodequad} generated by the same corotational
kinematics, this torque enters without double-counting the conservative
$Q_a$--$S_a$ coupling.

\subsection{Orbital dynamics}

The pair interaction energy truncated at monopole plus quadrupole order is
\begin{align}
U_{ab}
&=
-\frac{G M_a M_b}{d_{ab}}
\nonumber\\
&\quad
-\frac{3G}{2d_{ab}^3}
\Bigl(
M_b\,\hat D_{ab}^\top I_a \hat D_{ab}
+
M_a\,\hat D_{ab}^\top I_b \hat D_{ab}
\Bigr).
\label{eq:Uab}
\end{align}

This retains the monopole and monopole--quadrupole couplings, but neglects
quadrupole--quadrupole interactions of order $O(d_{ab}^{-5})$ and all higher
multipolar contributions.

The orbital equation is
\begin{equation}
M_a \ddot X_a
=
-\nabla_{X_a}\sum_{b<c} U_{bc}.
\label{eq:Xquad}
\end{equation}

Equations
\eqref{eq:Qdot},
\eqref{eq:Seq},
\eqref{eq:Zquad},
\eqref{eq:spinquad}, and
\eqref{eq:Xquad}
form a closed quadrupole tide model. Relative to the simpler reduced closure
that keeps only $J_a(S_a)\Omega_a$, the additional term \eqref{eq:Pimodequad}
is algebraically modest but physically important for obliquity evolution.

\subsection{First-order implementation form}

Using the auxiliary variable $W_a=D_t S_a$, the quadrupole subsystem may be
written in first-order form as
\begin{equation}
D_t S_a = W_a,
\label{eq:firstS}
\end{equation}
\begin{align}
D_t W_a
&=
-2\gamma_a W_a
-\omega_{a2}^2 S_a
-\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\mathrm{r},a}}
c_{an}(S_a-Z_{an})
\nonumber\\
&\quad
-\lambda_a E_{a,\mathrm{body}}
-\frac{1}{2}\tilde c_{J,a}\,
\mathrm{STF}
\bigl(
\Omega_a\Omega_a^\top
\bigr),
\label{eq:firstW}
\end{align}
together with \eqref{eq:Zquad}. This form is convenient for standard ODE
solvers. In particular, if one implements the inertial quadrupole as
$I_a=c_{Q,a}Q_aS_aQ_a^\top$ in the orbital force and pair potential,
then the same model must use the reciprocal coefficient
$\lambda_a=c_{Q,a}/2$ in \eqref{eq:Seq} and \eqref{eq:firstW}; otherwise the
bookkeeping of conservative energy is inconsistent. Likewise, the same
coefficient $\tilde c_{J,a}$ must appear both in the inertia tensor
\eqref{eq:JS} and in the rotational forcing term of \eqref{eq:Seq} and
\eqref{eq:firstW}. In the improved closure, the spin update should then be
performed from \eqref{eq:spinexpanded} with the explicit conservative torque
$N_{a,\mathrm{body}}$, the reciprocal dissipative torques
\eqref{eq:NdissW}--\eqref{eq:NdissZ}, and the modal angular-momentum term
\eqref{eq:Pimodequad}. This is still a modest implementation change because
$\dot S_a$, $\dot W_a$, and the branch lags $S_a-Z_{an}$ are already available
from the first-order state, so both $\dot\Pi_{S,a}$ in \eqref{eq:Pimodedot} and
the algebraic dissipative torques can be assembled at the same right-hand-side
evaluation. In the split extension below, the same update simply includes the
additional bulge-branch torque \eqref{eq:NdissB}. No additional conservative
storage term
proportional to $S_a:\mathrm{STF}(\Omega_a\Omega_a^\top)$ should be added on top of
$\tfrac12\Omega_a^\top J_a(S_a)\Omega_a$, because that Hamiltonian term
already contains the rotational-flattening coupling in this reduced closure.

\subsection{Conservative quadrupole energy}

In the nondissipative limit $\gamma_a=0$ and with all relaxation branches
removed, the natural mechanical energy of the quadrupole closure is
\begin{align}
E_{\mathrm{mech}}
=
&\sum_a \Biggl[
\frac{1}{2}M_a|\dot X_a|^2
+\frac{1}{2}\Omega_a^\top J_a(S_a)\Omega_a \nonumber\\
&\qquad\quad+\frac{1}{2}\|W_a\|^2
+\frac{1}{2}\omega_{a2}^2\|S_a\|^2
\Biggr]
\nonumber\\
&
+\sum_{a<b} U_{ab},
\label{eq:Emechquad}
\end{align}
up to the same truncation order used elsewhere in the reduced model and with
any chosen normalization of the STF norm. The rotational-flattening coupling
is already contained in $\frac{1}{2}\Omega_a^\top J_a(S_a)\Omega_a$ through the
shape dependence of $J_a(S_a)$. Therefore the reduced conservative diagnostic
must not add a second term proportional to
$S_a:\mathrm{STF}(\Omega_a\Omega_a^\top)$ unless the reduced spin equation is
simultaneously upgraded to evolve the corresponding extra contribution to the
body-frame angular momentum.


\section{Split rotational-bulge extension for obliquity damping}

The one-tensor quadrupole closure developed above formally conserves angular
momentum by retaining the modal contribution $\Pi_{S,a}$ and the explicit
quadrupole torque in the spin balance. Even so, a single STF tensor $S_a$ must
then represent both the tide-raised bulge and the rotationally supported
oblateness. These two mechanisms generally relax on different timescales and
need not remain aligned with the same direction, so a more flexible reduced
model separates them.

A simple next-level reduced model is therefore to split the quadrupole into a
\emph{tide-driven} STF tensor $S_a$ and a separate \emph{rotational bulge}
$B_a$, also taken symmetric and trace free. The total body-frame quadrupole is
then written as
\begin{equation}
I_{a,\mathrm{body}} = c_{Q,a}^{(t)} S_a + c_{Q,a}^{(r)} B_a,
\end{equation}
and the corresponding inertial quadrupole is therefore
\begin{equation}
I_a = Q_a\,I_{a,\mathrm{body}}\,Q_a^\top
= Q_a\bigl(c_{Q,a}^{(t)} S_a + c_{Q,a}^{(r)} B_a\bigr)Q_a^\top,
\label{eq:IquadSplit}
\end{equation}
while the body-frame spin inertia becomes
\begin{equation}
J_a(B_a) = J_{a0} I_3 - c_{J,a}^{(r)} B_a.
\label{eq:JSsplitB}
\end{equation}
In this split closure the tide-driven tensor $S_a$ contributes to the external
quadrupole but not directly to the reduced spin inertia. This avoids
reintroducing a separate conservative spin--shape coupling into the $S_a$
equation while keeping the rotational bulge $B_a$ as the explicit carrier of
spin-supported flattening.
The tide-driven quadrupole continues to satisfy the second-order rheological
shape equation,
\begin{align}
D_t^2 S_a
&+
2\gamma_a D_t S_a
+
\omega_{a2}^2 S_a
+
\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\mathrm{r},a}} c_{an}(S_a-Z_{an})
\nonumber\\
&= -\lambda_a E_{a,\mathrm{body}},
\label{eq:Seqsplit}
\end{align}
with the same branch equations
\begin{equation}
\tau_{an} D_t Z_{an} = S_a - Z_{an}.
\label{eq:Zsplit}
\end{equation}
The rotational bulge is promoted to its own reduced variable and evolved with a
first-order corotating relaxation law,
\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:Bquad}
\begin{align}
\tau_{B,a} D_t B_a + B_a &= -\eta_a\,\mathrm{STF}(\Omega_a\Omega_a^\top)
-\lambda_{B,a} E_{a,\mathrm{body}},
 \\
\lambda_{B,a}&=\eta_a\,\frac{c_{Q,a}^{(r)}}{c_{J,a}^{(r)}},
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
where $\tau_{B,a}$ is the relaxation time of the spin-supported figure and
$\eta_a>0$ controls its quasi-static amplitude. The additional reciprocal tidal
term is required if the same $B_a$ contributes both to the spin inertia and to
the external quadrupole: otherwise the orbital field can do conservative work on
$B_a$ through $I_a$ with no matching forcing in the $B_a$ equation. With the STF
sign convention used throughout this paper, the minus sign in the spin forcing is
required for a positive-spin-rate hydrostatic bulge to be oblate, i.e. compressed
along the spin axis and enlarged in the orthogonal directions. In the limit
$\tau_{B,a}\to 0$ the spin bulge approaches the instantaneous hydrostatic figure,
while finite $\tau_{B,a}$ allows the rotational bulge to lag behind changes in
spin rate, spin direction, or the body-frame tidal tensor. Because $B_a$ is
itself a shape tensor, the coefficient $c_{J,a}^{(r)}$ in~\eqref{eq:JSsplitB}
should be read as the geometric map from shape to spin inertia. It is typically
of the same order as the ordinary inertia-shape coefficient $c_{J,a}$. The extra
rotational-flattening strength that was merged into
$\tilde c_{J,a}=c_{J,a}+2\alpha_a$ in the one-tensor closure should here be
absorbed into the response amplitude $\eta_a$, not into an additional direct
$S_a$-dependence of the spin inertia.

For diagnostics and energy bookkeeping in the split closure, the stored
mechanical contribution of the first-order bulge branch is the non-negative
self-energy
\begin{equation}
\mathcal E_B
= \sum_a \frac{c_{J,a}^{(r)}}{4\eta_a}\,B_a:B_a.
\label{eq:EBsplit}
\end{equation}
No additional explicit $B_a:E_{a,\mathrm{body}}$ term should be added on top of
this: the conservative tidal coupling of $B_a$ is already present through the
total quadrupole in the pair interaction energy~\eqref{eq:Uab}, and adding it
again would double count the same interaction.

Matching the low-frequency visible equilibrium of the one-tensor model gives
$\eta_a \approx (c_{J,a}+2\alpha_a)/(2\omega_{a2}^2)$, while matching its
low-frequency phase lag gives $\tau_{B,a}\approx 2\gamma_a/\omega_{a2}^2$.

For bookkeeping, the non-negative dissipation rate of this first-order branch is
naturally governed by the lag away from the instantaneous target
$B_{a,*}=-\eta_a\,\mathrm{STF}(\Omega_a\Omega_a^\top)-\lambda_{B,a}E_{a,\mathrm{body}}$,
namely
\begin{equation}
\dot D_B
= \sum_a \frac{c_{J,a}^{(r)}}{2\eta_a\tau_{B,a}}
\,(B_a-B_{a,*}):(B_a-B_{a,*}).
\end{equation}
The recoverable mechanical energy is therefore the branch \emph{self-energy}
\eqref{eq:EBsplit}, not the lag energy. Equivalently, completing the square
around $B_{a,*}$ produces a lag-squared term plus a forcing-dependent offset.
Keeping only $(B_a-B_{a,*}):(B_a-B_{a,*})$ as a stored-energy diagnostic would
make the bookkeeping look artificially non-conservative in oblique or
precessing runs because $B_{a,*}$ changes as the body-frame tidal tensor and
spin direction evolve.

The spin equation is then upgraded only slightly:
\begin{align}
\frac{d}{dt}\Bigl(J_a(B_a)&\Omega_a + \Pi_{S,a}\Bigr)
+
\Omega_a\times\Bigl(J_a(B_a)\Omega_a + \Pi_{S,a}\Bigr)
=\nonumber \\
&N_{a,\mathrm{body}}
+
N^{\mathrm{diss}}_{W,a}
+
N^{\mathrm{diss}}_{Z,a}
+
N^{\mathrm{diss}}_{B,a},
\label{eq:spinquadSplit}
\end{align}
where $\Pi_{S,a}=2\,\operatorname{axl}([W_a,S_a])$ is still the explicit modal
angular momentum retained from the second-order tide-driven quadrupole, and the
first-order bulge branch contributes the reciprocal dissipative torque
\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:NdissB}
\begin{align}
N^{\mathrm{diss}}_{B,a}
&=
-\frac{c_{J,a}^{(r)}}{\eta_a}\,
\operatorname{axl}\!\bigl([B_{a,*}-B_a,B_a]\bigr),
 \\
B_{a,*}&=-\eta_a\,\mathrm{STF}(\Omega_a\Omega_a^\top)-\lambda_{B,a}E_{a,\mathrm{body}}.
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
The first-order variable $B_a$ does not introduce an additional second-order
modal kinetic term, so no separate $\Pi_{B,a}$ is required in this reduced
extension. The orbital force and body-frame torque are computed from the total
quadrupole \eqref{eq:IquadSplit} in the same way as before. In particular, the
tide-driven shape equation continues to use only the reciprocal coupling to the
tide-driven piece, namely $-\lambda_a E_{a,\mathrm{body}}$ with
$\lambda_a=c_{Q,a}^{(t)}/2$; the rotational bulge enters the orbital dynamics
and torque only through the total quadrupole $I_a$, the spin inertia
$J_a(B_a)$, and the branch torque $N^{\mathrm{diss}}_{B,a}$ associated with its
corotational relaxation.

This split extension remains simple compared with the rest of the model. It
adds only one extra STF tensor and one extra relaxation timescale per body, and
it does not alter the pairwise $O(N^2)$ force evaluation. The expected benefit
is specifically in spin-axis evolution: the torque acting on the separately
relaxing rotational bulge can dissipate precessional energy even when the
tide-driven quadrupole is dominated by the line-of-centers forcing. That makes
robust obliquity damping a more realistic outcome of the reduced model.

\section{Relation to Real Problems}

\subsection{What remains toy-like}

The model retains the key qualitative features of tidal evolution---full
three-dimensional orbital motion, explicit tidal multipoles and their
backreaction, spin--orbit coupling, rotational flattening, and
frequency-dependent dissipative lag---while remaining in ODE form. This
suffices to reproduce the leading-order structure of spin synchronization,
orbital circularization, and obliquity damping qualitatively. However,
several important simplifications remain.

The interior is not evolved hydrodynamically, and real planets may contain
cores, mantles, oceans, and layered viscosities that are not represented
here. The rheological coefficients $\gamma_a$, $c_{an}$, and $\tau_{an}$ are
effective reduced parameters representing a linearized frequency response, not
a detailed microphysical material law. The model retains only the leading
quadrupole angular-momentum correction $\Pi_{S,a}$; higher-order modal
contributions and a fully systematic continuum angular-momentum closure are
still omitted, so exact global angular-momentum conservation holds only within
the retained truncation order. The linearized inertia \eqref{eq:JS} is
intended for weak to moderate deformations; very strong tides would require
higher modes and nonlinear corrections. Mechanical dissipation is included,
but heating, melting, convection, and feedback on material parameters are not.

\subsection{Comparison with common tide models}

A single damping term proportional to $D_t S_a$ resembles a constant
time-lag description. By contrast, the branch variables $Z_{an}$ allow a
frequency-dependent lag more like a generalized Maxwell or anelastic
response. This is still a reduced rheology, but it is more flexible and more
realistic than a single constant-lag parameter.

\subsection{Relation to Love number and quality factor}

Planetary tidal dissipation is often parameterized by the quadrupolar Love
number $k_2$ and the quality factor $Q$. In the present reduced model, the
more fundamental objects are the linear response coefficients appearing in
\eqref{eq:Seq}. For a harmonic forcing at angular frequency $\sigma$ in the
body frame, one may write the complex quadrupole response schematically as
\begin{equation}
\widetilde S_a(\sigma)
=
-\chi_a(\sigma)
\widetilde F_a(\sigma),
\label{eq:chiDef}
\end{equation}
where $\widetilde F_a$ denotes the STF forcing from the tidal tensor and the
rotational term, and where
\begin{equation}
\chi_a(\sigma)
=
\Biggl[
-\sigma^2
+2i\gamma_a\sigma
+\omega_{a2}^2
+\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\mathrm{r},a}}
c_{an}
\left(
1-\frac{1}{1+i\sigma\tau_{an}}
\right)
\Biggr]^{-1}
\label{eq:chiModel}
\end{equation}
in the linearized frequency-domain approximation. The static compliance
$\chi_a(0)$ sets the scale of the equilibrium quadrupole and therefore of an
effective static $k_2$, while the ratio of imaginary to real parts of
$\chi_a(\sigma)$ determines the phase lag and hence an effective
$Q^{-1}(\sigma)$.

The present parameters $(\gamma_a,\omega_{a2},c_{an},\tau_{an})$ should
therefore be viewed as a way to fit or calibrate the complex quadrupolar response of a
body over the frequency range of interest, rather than as a unique microscopic
material model. With one branch the model resembles a simple constant-lag-like
response over a limited band, whereas several branches can be chosen to better
match a desired frequency-dependent $k_2(\sigma)$ and $Q(\sigma)$.

\section{Implementation and Computational Remarks}

The upgraded quadrupole model remains inexpensive compared with full
hydrodynamics. For $N$ bodies, the dominant cost of each right-hand-side
evaluation is the pairwise gravitational and tidal interaction, scaling as
$O(N^2)$. The additional costs from the refinements are all lower order:
building and inverting the $3\times3$ inertia tensor $J_a(S_a)$ is $O(N)$,
the rotational-flattening term is $O(N)$, and $N_{\mathrm{r},a}$ rheology
branches add $O(NN_{\mathrm{r}})$ state variables and arithmetic. The main
effect of the improvements is therefore a modest increase in state size and
per-body algebra, not a qualitative change in complexity.

The accompanying reference code integrates the quadrupole closure directly.
For body $a$ the evolved state is
\begin{equation}
\bigl(
X_a,\dot X_a,q_a,\Omega_a,S_a,W_a,Z_{a1},\dots,Z_{aN_{\mathrm{r},a}}
\bigr),
\end{equation}
with an additional split-bulge tensor $B_a$ when the split rotational-bulge
extension is enabled. Here $q_a$ is a unit quaternion representing the body
orientation.

The key numerical choices are the following:
\begin{itemize}
\item orientations are advanced with quaternions and renormalized as needed,
\item $S_a$, $W_a$, $Z_{an}$, and when present $B_a$ are stored as full
$3\times 3$ matrices and reprojected onto their symmetric trace-free parts to
suppress numerical drift,
\item the orbital force, pair potential, and quadrupole torque are assembled
from the same truncated potential, while the spin equation also retains the
quadrupole-mode angular momentum term
$\Pi_{S,a}=2\,\operatorname{axl}([W_a,S_a])$ together with the reciprocal
dissipative torques generated by the active relaxation variables,
\item the smallest eigenvalue of the shape-dependent inertia tensor is watched
throughout the run so that the evolution stays in the weak-deformation regime;
in the one-tensor closure,
$J_a(S_a)=J_{a0}I_3-\tilde c_{J,a}S_a$, so one must keep
$\tilde c_{J,a}\lambda_{\max}(S_a)<J_{a0}$.
\end{itemize}

A straightforward numerical realization stores each STF quadrupole variable
$S_a$, $W_a$, and $Z_{an}$ as a full $3\times 3$ matrix rather than in a
minimal five-component basis. This is slightly redundant, but it keeps the
ODEs close to their analytical form: the corotational terms remain simple
commutators, and the rotational forcing is just
$\mathrm{STF}(\Omega_a\Omega_a^\top)$. Numerical drift can then be controlled
by re-projecting these matrices onto their symmetric trace-free parts after
right-hand-side evaluations or at output times.

The orientation may be represented by unit quaternions $q_a$ mapping
body-frame vectors into inertial-frame vectors. For moderate $N$, the orbital
force, body-frame tidal tensor, and diagnostic quadrupole torque can all be
assembled by vectorized pairwise operations, preserving the $O(N^2)$ cost of
the reduced model. For the particular leading-order closure used here, it is
also convenient in the spin update to solve the $3\times 3$ linear system
\begin{equation}
J_a(S_a)\dot\Omega_a
=
-
\dot J_a(S_a)\Omega_a
-
\Omega_a\times\bigl(J_a(S_a)\Omega_a\bigr)
\end{equation}
directly, rather than forming $J_a(S_a)^{-1}$ explicitly.

For generalized rheology, initializing each branch near $Z_{an}=S_a$ avoids an
artificial start-up transient. The reference implementation follows the
improved quadrupole closure adopted in this paper: it keeps the deformation
dependence of $J_a(S_a)$, retains the leading quadrupole angular momentum
$\Pi_{S,a}=2\,\operatorname{axl}([W_a,S_a])$, and includes the torque
\eqref{eq:Nbody} explicitly in the spin balance. The conservative diagnostic
should still be constructed from the same truncated reduced energy used by the
ODEs, with the same coefficient $\tilde c_{J,a}$ appearing in both
$J_a(S_a)$ and the rotational forcing term. No additional
$S_a:\mathrm{STF}(\Omega_a\Omega_a^\top)$ storage term should be added on top of
$\frac{1}{2}\Omega_a^\top J_a(S_a)\Omega_a$ in this closure. After that
correction, remaining small drift in practical integrations is primarily
numerical and reflects the use of finite tolerances, post-processed
dissipation integrals, and the residual truncation error of the reduced model
rather than an omitted conservative energy reservoir. In production runs it is also worth monitoring the smallest eigenvalue of
$J_a(S_a)$ and rejecting parameter choices or time steps that drive the
inertia tensor close to singularity.

\section{Illustrative Double-Planet Evolution}

To illustrate the qualitative behavior of the quadrupole model, this section
presents two representative integrations for close double-planet systems. Both
use the same orbital and spin configuration but differ in the material
parameters of the planets. Together they exhibit qualitatively distinct
regimes of tidal evolution: smooth secular decay driven by a quasi-static
bulge, and impulsive dissipation concentrated in a pair of resonance-passage
events.

\subsection{Smooth dissipative evolution}

Figure~\ref{fig:double-planet-tidal} shows an integration starting from
a randomly initialized eccentric, oblique, asynchronously rotating
configuration with non-zero initial quadrupole deformation. The figure is
not intended as a parameter survey, but as a compact diagnostic of how the
reduced model moves energy and angular momentum between orbit, spin, and
tidal shape variables during long-term evolution.

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{Double planet tidal.jpg}
\caption{Smooth dissipative double-planet integration with the split
quadrupole model. Both planets start from a non-zero deformation and relax
rapidly into the quasi-static tidal regime, after which energy is steadily
drained from the orbit by the lagged quadrupole. The compensated
mechanical-plus-dissipated energy (red) remains flat to within well under
one percent of the mechanical energy scale throughout.}
\label{fig:double-planet-tidal}
\end{figure*}

The upper-left panel shows the energy bookkeeping. The mechanical energy
(green dashed) and the cumulative dissipated energy (black) each vary by
roughly 15\% of the initial orbital energy over the course of the run, yet
their compensated sum (red) remains essentially flat throughout---its total
variation is well under one percent of the mechanical energy scale. That
near-constancy confirms that the reduced ODE closure is accounting for
dissipated energy consistently rather than leaking it into truncated degrees
of freedom.

The upper-right panel reveals a striking asymmetry between the two bodies.
Planet~2's squared deformation rate $\|W\|_F^2$ and lag $\|S-Z\|_F^2$ both
spike to roughly $0.10$ at the start, then collapse by two to three orders of
magnitude within the first $\sim\!100$ time units. Planet~1's analogous
quantities are so small they barely register on the same scale. The burst for
planet~2 is essentially a rapid re-equilibration from an initial state far from
tidal equilibrium. After that transient, both bodies settle into the
slowly-evolving quasi-static regime in which the quadrupole closely tracks its
instantaneous equilibrium value.

The lower-left panel shows the secular orbital evolution. Both the eccentricity
and the semi-major axis decrease monotonically, and their curves run roughly
parallel throughout, reflecting the coupled circularization--inspiral expected
from tidal dissipation. The decay is not uniform: there is a noticeable
acceleration around $t \approx 4000$ where both curves steepen before settling
into a final decline.

The lower-right panel compares the spin angular frequencies of the two planets
with the orbital mean motion. Both spin rates are nearly constant on the scale
of the plot; any spin-down is small compared with the rise in orbital mean
motion as the orbit contracts. The approach toward synchronization is therefore
driven primarily by orbital inspiral rather than by spin-down. Planet~2,
spinning at $\sim\!0.84$, ends the run at roughly twice the final orbital
frequency---substantially closer to synchronization than planet~1, which spins
at $\sim\!1.19$, or nearly three times the final orbital mean motion. The
spin-relaxation timescale evidently exceeds the integration window by a
considerable margin.

\subsection{Resonance-passage evolution}

Figure~\ref{fig:curious-double-planet} shows a second integration run with
the same orbital and spin initial conditions but with both planets initialized
at zero deformation ($S_0 = W_0 = 0$) and with higher elastic mode stiffnesses.
The resulting dynamics are qualitatively different and considerably more
structured.

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{Curious Double Planet.jpg}
\caption{Resonance-passage double-planet integration. Both planets are
initialized with zero quadrupole deformation. The run reveals two isolated
bursts of internal quadrupole activity---at $t\approx 1000$ (planet~1
dominant, blue) and $t\approx 4500$ (planet~2 dominant, orange)---each
accompanied by a sudden step-down in eccentricity and a corresponding jump
in cumulative dissipation. Between these events the system evolves
quasi-statically and the spin rates remain essentially frozen. The
compensated energy (red, upper-left) is flat throughout, confirming that
the impulsive events are physically captured by the reduced ODE closure
rather than being numerical artifacts.}
\label{fig:curious-double-planet}
\end{figure*}

Because both planets start from zero deformation, there is no initial
equilibration transient: the quadrupole activity begins near zero and stays
there until the first event. The evolution then proceeds in three distinct
phases separated by the two bursts.

\textbf{Between events, quasi-static evolution.}
During the long quiet intervals---$t \lesssim 900$ and $1200 \lesssim t
\lesssim 4300$---the reduced dynamics are indistinguishable from ordinary
quasi-static tidal friction. The bulge tracks the instantaneous tidal
forcing closely ($\|W\|_F^2$ and $\|S-Z\|_F^2$ both small), the orbit
circularizes and contracts gradually, and the spin rates are essentially
constant. This is the regime captured by the smooth run above, but here
the interlude is interrupted.

\textbf{Resonance passages.}
As the orbit slowly contracts, the orbital mean motion $n$ increases.
Because the orbital forcing of a tidally deformable body contains harmonics
of the mean motion, the effective forcing frequency near the body's natural
quadrupole frequency $\omega_{a2}$ sweeps upward in time. When a harmonic
$k n$ passes through $\omega_{a2}$, the quadrupole mode is briefly driven
near resonance: the deformation amplitude rises, dissipation intensifies,
and angular momentum is exchanged between the orbit and the internal
degrees of freedom before the mode is detuned by the resulting orbital
change. The event is self-limiting---the orbital shift moves the system
off resonance---leaving a permanent step-down in eccentricity and semi-major
axis. Resonance sweeping of this kind is a recognized mechanism in celestial
mechanics, though its occurrence in a reduced ODE model
of this type is instructive in its own right.

In the present run, planet~1 ($\omega_{12} = 4.54$) undergoes its passage
first, at $t \approx 1000$. The burst in $\|W\|_F^2$ for planet~1 (blue,
upper-right panel) is large and multi-peaked, indicating that the system
oscillates through the resonance over several orbital periods before settling.
Planet~2 ($\omega_{22} = 5.06$) resonates later, at $t \approx 4500$, and
the corresponding orange burst is similarly structured. The two mode
frequencies are close but distinct, so the passages occur at different times
as the mean motion climbs through the relevant harmonic.

Each event has a clear signature in all four panels simultaneously: a burst
in the quadrupole activity (upper-right), a step in cumulative dissipation
and a corresponding drop in mechanical energy (upper-left), and step-down
features in both eccentricity and semi-major axis (lower-left). The fact
that all four panels are consistent is a non-trivial check: the impulsive
events are not numerical artifacts but are physically coherent within the
reduced ODE closure.

\textbf{Spin evolution.}
The lower-right panel shows that both spin rates are essentially frozen
throughout, including during the bursts. The spin-down from 3.73 to 3.44
(planet~1) and 2.63 to 2.42 (planet~2) measured at the end of the run
represents a change of roughly 8\% in both cases over the full integration,
but this change is spread so uniformly that it is imperceptible on the
scale of the panel. The orbital mean motion, by contrast, rises visibly.
The resonance passages do not cause detectable kicks to the spin rate,
consistent with the expectation that the torque impulse during a short
resonance event is much smaller than the angular momentum stored in the spin.

\textbf{Energy bookkeeping.}
The upper-left panel confirms that the conserved mechanical-plus-dissipated
sum (red) is flat throughout---including during both burst events---to within
the numerical precision of the run. The total dissipated energy reaches
$8.9 \times 10^{-3}$, split roughly evenly between the two events and the
quiet intervals. This is a more stringent test of the reduced closure than
the smooth run, because the impulsive events require the energy bookkeeping
to remain consistent during rapid transient dynamics rather than only in the
quasi-static limit.

Taken together, the two integrations show that the reduced quadrupole ODE
closure is capable of representing qualitatively different regimes of tidal
evolution within the same framework, and that the energy bookkeeping remains
mechanically consistent in both the smooth and impulsive cases.

\section{Model verification}

The reference implementation includes a regression-style driver
\texttt{run\_tests()} that integrates representative two-body problems,
reconstructs derived orbital and spin diagnostics from the saved trajectory,
and then applies explicit quantitative pass/fail thresholds. Each case also
writes a diagnostic plot. The purpose of the suite is not to calibrate the
model against a particular planetary system, but to verify that the reduced ODE
closure behaves in the limits it was designed to capture: the null Kepler
limit, conservative equilibrium, quasi-static response, rotational
flattening, rheological distinguishability, secular circularization, spin
synchronization, and obliquity damping.

\subsection{Implemented checks}

\paragraph{01\_null\_kepler\_limit}
This case verifies that the code reduces to ordinary two-body Kepler motion
when all quadrupole couplings are turned off.
\par\smallskip
\textbf{Pass criteria.} Maximum drift in eccentricity $<5\times10^{-6}$,
maximum drift in semi-major axis $<5\times10^{-6}$, and maximum drift in the
conservative energy diagnostic $<5\times10^{-7}$.
\par\smallskip
\textbf{Current result.} All three margins are passed comfortably, with
$\max|\Delta e|=3.674\times10^{-9}$,
$\max|\Delta a|=1.790\times10^{-8}$, and
$\max|\Delta E_{\mathrm{total}}|=7.308\times10^{-10}$.

\paragraph{02\_conservative\_equilibrium}
This case checks that a circular synchronized configuration initialized near
the reduced static quadrupole equilibrium remains there in the nondissipative
limit.
\par\smallskip
\textbf{Pass criteria.} Maximum total-energy drift $<2\times10^{-5}$,
maximum eccentricity $<2\times10^{-3}$, and mean bulge--line-of-centers angle
$<2^\circ$.
\par\smallskip
\textbf{Current result.} The run passes by a wide margin with
$\max|\Delta E_{\mathrm{total}}|=1.019\times10^{-14}$,
$e_{\max}=2.862\times10^{-7}$, and mean bulge angle $0.579^\circ$.

\paragraph{03\_quasi\_static\_bulge}
This case verifies that strongly damped quadrupoles track the instantaneous
hydrostatic tide in the slow-forcing regime.
\par\smallskip
\textbf{Pass criteria.} Mean relative shape error in the late-time part of the
run $<0.20$, final relative shape error $<0.10$, and mean bulge lag
$<5^\circ$.
\par\smallskip
\textbf{Current result.} The implementation passes with mean and final
relative shape errors both $8.449\times10^{-3}$ and a mean bulge lag of
$0.393^\circ$.

\paragraph{04\_rotational\_flattening}
This case isolates the spin-supported oblateness mechanism and confirms that
the shape equation reproduces the expected oblate equilibrium when the tidal
field is negligible.
\par\smallskip
\textbf{Pass criteria.} Relative error to the isolated rotational equilibrium
$<0.10$ and the presence of an oblate STF eigenvalue pattern.
\par\smallskip
\textbf{Current result.} The run passes with relative error
$3.321\times10^{-9}$ and eigenvalues
$[-0.0693,\,0.0347,\,0.0347]$, as expected for an oblate figure.

\paragraph{05\_rheology\_comparison}
This comparison confirms that the direct viscous closure and a delayed
Maxwell-like branch closure are observationally distinguishable in the same
orbital setup. The code implements this as two companion runs,
\texttt{05A\_rheology\_viscous} and \texttt{05B\_rheology\_maxwell}.
\par\smallskip
\textbf{Pass criteria.} The absolute difference in mean bulge lag must exceed
$0.5^\circ$, and the relative difference in final cumulative dissipation must
exceed $5\%$.
\par\smallskip
\textbf{Current result.} The lag difference is $27.174^\circ$ and the relative
dissipation difference is $78.74\%$, with the viscous run giving a mean lag of
$2.689^\circ$ and final cumulative dissipation $8.518119\times10^{-5}$, while
the Maxwell-like run gives a mean lag of $29.863^\circ$ and final cumulative
dissipation $1.810825\times10^{-5}$.

\paragraph{06\_circularization}
This case verifies the expected secular dissipative trend in an eccentric
binary with active tides.
\par\smallskip
\textbf{Pass criteria.} Final eccentricity below the initial value, final
semi-major axis below the initial value, and relative drift in the compensated
energy diagnostic below $2\%$.
\par\smallskip
\textbf{Current result.} The orbit evolves from $e_0=0.4500$ to
$e_f=0.4375$ and from $a_0=2.6000$ to $a_f=2.5478$, while the relative drift of
mechanical plus dissipated energy is numerically zero to the reported
precision.

\paragraph{07\_spin\_synchronization}
This case confirms that a super-synchronous spin is torqued toward the orbital
frequency when a persistent tidal lag is present.
\par\smallskip
\textbf{Pass criteria.} The monitored spin ratio must decrease, the final
value must be closer to $1$ than the initial one, the mean bulge lag must
exceed $0.2^\circ$, and the minimum spin ratio over the run must fall below the
initial value.
\par\smallskip
\textbf{Current result.} The tracked ratio decreases from $4.0000$ to
$3.9820$, the minimum value is $3.9820$, and the mean bulge lag is
$2.132^\circ$.

\paragraph{08\_obliquity\_survey}
This survey tests whether the model produces net obliquity damping across a
small grid of tilted initial states and damping strengths rather than only in a
single hand-picked example. The implementation surveys the grid
$\{15^\circ,45^\circ,75^\circ\}\times\{0.10,0.30,0.60\}$ and measures the
change in the spin--orbit angle over each run.
\par\smallskip
\textbf{Pass criteria.} At least half of the survey cases must have
$\Delta\theta_{\mathrm{spin-orbit}}<0$, and the median
$\Delta\theta_{\mathrm{spin-orbit}}$ over the grid must also be negative.
\par\smallskip
\textbf{Current result.} The passing fraction is $0.667$ and the median change
is $-0.0270^\circ$. Obliquity damping is therefore present across most, though not
all, of the sampled parameter box.

\subsection{Current status of the model}

At present the suite reports \emph{Passed 8 / 8 tests}. The nondissipative
checks are particularly strong: the null-Kepler and conservative-equilibrium
cases show only tiny residual drifts, at the level of $10^{-9}$ to $10^{-14}$
in the reported diagnostics. The quasi-static and rotational-flattening tests
also indicate that the retained STF shape equations reproduce their intended
instantaneous equilibria with substantial margin.

The dissipative tests show the expected qualitative behavior of the reduced
model without spoiling the compensated energy bookkeeping. In the present run
set the code clearly distinguishes viscous and Maxwell-like responses,
produces secular circularization and inspiral in eccentric systems, drives a
super-synchronous spin in the correct direction, and yields net obliquity
damping in a majority of the sampled survey cases. The last point should still
be read as a trend rather than a theorem: the current survey indicates that the
model is capable of obliquity damping over a useful region of parameter space,
not that every admissible parameter choice must damp monotonically.

\section{Conclusion}

A reduced Newtonian model of tidally deformable pseudo-fluid planets has been
constructed in two layers. The first is a full modal framework with arbitrary
angular modes, body-frame rotation, deformation-dependent spin inertia, a
reduced rotational-flattening coupling implemented through the
shape-dependent spin inertia, and generalized rheology. The second is a quadrupole implementation model
suitable for direct numerical integration.

Relative to the simplest damped-quadrupole tide model, the present version is
improved in four respects: it allows the spin inertia to vary with
the deformation, it includes rotational flattening through the same reduced
spin--shape coefficient that enters the spin kinetic energy, it supports
frequency-dependent lag through internal relaxation branches, and it retains
the leading angular momentum carried by the quadrupole mode itself. This last
correction is small enough to preserve the basic ODE structure of the model,
yet important enough to restore the explicit quadrupole torque in the spin
balance and thereby improve the physical credibility of obliquity evolution.

The model remains a reduced model and not a full geophysical simulator.
Nevertheless, it captures the key orbit--spin--shape couplings needed for
tidal bulges, lag, synchronization, and orbital circularization. The standard
one-tensor quadrupole closure also provides a mechanically consistent reduced
description of spin evolution, including the leading modal angular momentum
carried by the quadrupole. For applications in which secular obliquity damping
is a primary target, however, the split-tensor extension
\eqref{eq:IquadSplit}--\eqref{eq:spinquadSplit} supplies the additional
orientational degrees of freedom associated with distinct tidal and rotational
bulges. The framework is therefore a useful intermediate model between
point-mass celestial mechanics and full interior simulation.
